SECTION 1

Before you start

What this guide covers, the shape of the business it describes, and how to read it.

1.1  What this guide is, and who it is for

This is the working manual for AWANZ POS by CloudChaserz — the point-of-sale, warehouse and head-office platform running at https://cloudchaserz.frappe.cloud. It is written to be used at the counter: every procedure is a numbered list you can follow with a customer standing in front of you.

Four audiences, four sections. Store associates need Section 4. Store managers need Section 4 and Section 5. The warehouse admin in Houston needs Section 6. Head office needs Section 7. Everyone needs Sections 2, 3, 8 and 9 — the logins, the URLs, the rewards programme and what to do when something goes wrong.

1.2  The estate in one paragraph

CloudChaserz runs eleven stores — one in Houston (Montrose, which is also the head-office store) and ten across the Tulsa metro and wider Oklahoma — plus one main warehouse, HOU-WH, at the Houston head office. All of them run on a single cloud site. A store sells; the warehouse ships to stores and never sells. Head office watches the chain from the Command dashboard. Customers can also buy online at /shop and collect in a store. Everything the customer or an associate reads — the wordmark, the product name, the rewards copy — comes from the tenant's brand settings, which is why every screen says CloudChaserz.

1.3  Conventions used here

  • A path in this typeface is a URL on the site, an e-mail address, a document type or a field name. Type it exactly as printed.
  • “Tap” is used throughout because the till is a touchscreen; on a laptop it means click.
  • A store code such as OK-BIX identifies a store everywhere in the system — in logins, in stock records, in the dashboard and on shipping labels.
  • Store, not “boutique”. Some older screens and reports still carry the word boutique in field names; it means the same thing.
  • Money is US dollars, times are America/Chicago, and every store's day boundary is the site's, not the device's.
A NOTE ON ACCURACY

Every credential, PIN, URL, threshold and screen name in this guide was taken from the platform's own source and from the final acceptance run carried out on the live site on 24 August 2026 (124 of 124 checks passed). Where a behaviour has a limit or a caveat, it is printed with the behaviour rather than left out.

2.5  What the PIN is, and what happens when it is wrong

  • The password signs you in to the site. The PIN unlocks the till for one person, so every sale carries the name of who rang it.
  • PINs are 4 to 6 digits and are stored hashed. Nobody — not a manager, not head office — can read a PIN back out of the system; a forgotten PIN is reset, never looked up.
  • Five wrong attempts locks the PIN. The screen then says “PIN locked after too many failed attempts; ask a manager to reset it” — which is a different message from “incorrect PIN”. A manager clears it with a reset (5.6).
  • Guessing is also rate limited: 20 attempts per associate per five minutes.

2.6  Security — read before the first real sale

⚠ RE-CREDENTIAL THIS SITE BEFORE REAL TRADING

The passwords and PINs in this guide are shared constants that ship with the demo seed and are the same on every CloudChaserz site. Anyone who knows a person's role knows their credential. Before the site takes a real customer's money or holds a real customer's details:

  1. Give every person a new password. Head office resets them from the admin desk (/app → User), or each person sets their own from the forgot-password link.
  2. Give every associate a new PIN — a manager can do this for their own store's associates from the POS (see 5.6); head office can do it for anyone.
  3. Change the site administrator password and remove any account nobody uses.
  4. Delete or disable the four demo shopper and test-customer accounts so they cannot be used to place orders.
  5. Confirm the tax rates with the CPA before the first live sale — see the warning in Appendix A.

The full permission model — who may do what, what is deliberately not locked down, and why — is documented in docs/security.md in the platform repository. Read it before granting anyone the Head Office role.

Three things the platform already does for you

  • A store manager cannot promote themselves. The role, the store and the user on an associate record are protected fields; an attempt to change them over the API returns 403, and nobody can grant a rank above their own.
  • A store user cannot read another store's data. Every endpoint checks the caller's store, and so do desk list views and single-document reads. A manager asking for another store's invoices gets an empty list, not somebody else's numbers.
  • PIN hashes are unreadable. They are encrypted out of the table entirely, so they cannot be listed, filtered on, or taken away to be cracked offline.

Sign in at https://cloudchaserz.frappe.cloud/login with your e-mail and password. Whoever you are, you land on /start. That is deliberate: the underlying framework would otherwise drop a shop-floor associate into the accounting desk. /start is a branded launcher that lists exactly the screens your account may open, names the store you work in, and carries “Powered by Futonix.com” at the foot.

Teach staff one URL: https://cloudchaserz.frappe.cloud/start. Add it to the home screen of the till. Everything else is one tap from there.

SECTION 4

Associate — how to

The counter, start to finish. Follow these with a customer in front of you.

4.1  Open the till

  1. Open https://cloudchaserz.frappe.cloud/start and sign in with your e-mail and password (cloud123 on this training site). Tap Point of sale.
  2. The first time on a new device, pick your store from the list. The device remembers it.
  3. Wait for the catalogue to load. It downloads once and is then cached on the device — around 160 items with prices, tax and reward tiers. You will see the tiles fill in.
  4. Tap your own name in the associate list. Not a colleague's — the PIN belongs to the name.
  5. Key your 4-digit PIN. The till unlocks on the Sell screen.
CLOCK IN AT THE SAME TIME

The unlock screen has a three-way switch: Unlock · Clock in · Clock out. Choose Clock in before keying the PIN and the same PIN both starts your shift and opens the till. See 4.11.

4.2  Ring a sale

  1. Add the items. Tap a tile, use the search box (Search name, code or serial), filter by department, or scan the barcode (4.3).
  2. Change the quantity on the basket line, or give a line discount by tapping the amount field on the line.
  3. If a promotion is running it applies automatically and shows in the Promotions chip. Type or scan a coupon code in the same sheet.
  4. Attach the customer if they are a member (4.5) — do this before you take payment, or the points are lost for that sale.
  5. Tap the total to go to Pay.

4.3  Scan a barcode

Two ways, and both land in whatever screen is open — Sell, Returns, Cycle count or Receive.

  • A Bluetooth or USB scanner (Socket Mobile S740, Zebra CS6080, Inateck and the like) types the code straight into the till. Nothing to press: just scan.
  • The device camera — tap the scan button and hold the code in the frame.

The till understands four kinds of label: EAN-13 on an item ticket, Code-128 on a serial label, a QR on a customer's card (adds the client), and the QR on a receipt (opens that sale in Returns).

IF THE SCAN TYPES INTO THE SEARCH BOX

The wedge listener deliberately ignores scans while the cursor is in a text field. Tap somewhere outside the box and scan again.

4.4  Age-verify a 21+ item

Every product in a restricted group is flagged. The moment you add one, the till stops and shows CHECK ID · 21+ — before the item reaches the basket. Nothing is sold until it passes.

  1. Ask for the customer's driver's licence or state ID.
  2. Scan ID — scan the PDF417 barcode on the back of the licence. The date of birth, the expiry, the initials and the issuing state are read on the device.
  3. Or Enter DOB — read the date of birth off the ID yourself and key it, with the expiry if you have it and the customer's two initials.
  4. The result is one of: Verified (the item goes in the basket) · Underage · Expired · Unreadable · Declined.
  5. Underage or expired means refused. Say so plainly and remove the item. Do not sell it.
WHAT THE SYSTEM KEEPS — AND WHAT IT DOES NOT

The record stores only the outcome: verified or not, the reason, whether you scanned or keyed it, the two initials, the issuing state, the store, who checked and when. The barcode payload, the full name, the licence number, the address and the exact date of birth are never written to the database. Tell customers that if they ask — it is printed on the screen under the scan box.

The receipt prints ID CHECKED · 21+ VERIFIED, and the client display shows “Please present your ID” while the check is open. The minimum age is 21, everywhere, regardless of state law.

The age gate also runs offline. If the network is down the check still happens on the device and the audit record is written when the sale syncs. There is no way to sell a 21+ item without it.

4.5  Attach a client by number or phone

  1. On the basket, tap the client area to open the keypad, or tap Client in the bottom bar.
  2. Key the customer's client number — six digits, printed on their receipt as MC###### — or their mobile number. Or scan the QR on their card.
  3. Or search by name, client number, mobile or e-mail on the Client screen and tap Attach to sale.
  4. The basket now shows their name, their tier and their point balance, and the reward tiers they can afford appear at Pay.
  5. Not a member yet? Tap New client and take their name, mobile and e-mail — see Section 8.

A search needs at least three characters. You can find a customer who normally shops at another store — the chain is one customer book — but a bulk list of the chain's customers is not something a store login can pull.

4.6  Take cash, card, or a split

Cash

  1. Tap Cash. Key what the customer handed you in Cash taken.
  2. The till shows the Change to give back. Tap Complete cash sale.

Card

  1. Tap Card. The amount goes to the Verifone V660p reader paired to this device.
  2. Ask the customer to tap, insert or swipe. Do not hand them your phone — the reader is the customer-facing device.
  3. Wait for the approval. The tender is stored with the card brand, the last four digits and the approval reference.

Split

  1. Tap Split. Key the cash part first; the till shows what is left.
  2. Take the remainder on the card. The invoice books two payment rows that add up to the total.
IF THE CUSTOMER IS REDEEMING A REWARD

The Pay screen shows only the tiers they can actually afford — $5 off at 100 points, $10 at 200, $15 at 300 — and how far away the next one is. One reward per transaction. The reward comes off the total after tax.

4.7  Print and e-mail the receipt

  1. The receipt prints by itself as soon as the sale completes. The till tries, in order: the reader's own printer, then the counter printer over the store network, then the browser's print dialog. If one route fails it falls through to the next.
  2. To reprint, tap Print on the receipt screen.
  3. To e-mail it, type the address in E-mail address on the same screen and send. The customer gets a link to the public receipt.
  4. The customer can also scan the QR on the paper receipt, which opens the same page — their lines, their points, their balance, and a private 1–5 rating of the visit.

4.8  Take a return

  1. From the Sell screen open Returns.
  2. Find the sale — scan the QR on their receipt, type the invoice number, or search the customer by name, phone or client number.
  3. Tick the lines coming back. Set the quantity (or pick the serial), then a reason (Change of mind, Defect, Sizing, Gift return, Other) and a condition: Sellable goes back on the floor, Damaged goes to the store's damaged stock and cannot be sold again.
  4. Choose the refund: Original card (only if the sale was paid by card, and never more than was charged), Cash from the drawer, or Store credit (needs a named customer).
  5. If the refund is over $2,500 including tax, or the sale is more than 30 days old, the till opens the Manager PIN sheet. Fetch a manager. If a manager is the one signed in, it is approved automatically.
  6. Confirm. The credit note is created, the money goes back, and the return receipt prints with a RETURN banner, the original sale, the approver and the reason.

What happens behind the scenes: the points the customer earned on those lines disappear — the balance never goes below zero — and the commission reverses against whoever originally sold it, not against you.

4.9  Do an exchange

  1. Start exactly as a return: find the sale and tick the lines coming back (4.8, steps 1–3).
  2. Instead of choosing a refund, tap Exchange. The selected lines carry across.
  3. Pick the new items from the catalogue on the Exchange screen.
  4. The till works out the settlement itself and shows one of three things:
    • Client pays — take the difference on card or cash.
    • Refund to client — give the remainder back on card, cash or store credit.
    • Even exchange — nothing changes hands.
  5. One tap writes both documents. The exchange window is 60 days; past that, a manager PIN.

4.10  Work offline — and what the queue means

The till is built to keep trading when the internet is not there. This is normal, not an emergency.

What still works

  • The whole catalogue, prices, tax and tiles — they are cached on the device.
  • Scanning, discounts, promotions already loaded, and the 21+ age gate.
  • Taking cash, completing the sale, and printing the receipt.

What does not

  • Charging a card on the reader, clocking in or out, looking up a sale for a return, searching for a customer who is not already on the screen, and web orders. All of these need the server.

What to do

  1. The top bar turns to OFFLINE. Carry on selling; take cash.
  2. Each completed sale is written into the device's queue and the bar reads OFFLINE · 1 QUEUED, then 2, and so on.
  3. The receipt still prints. The QR link on it will not open until that sale has synced.
  4. When the connection comes back the queue sends itself. Open Queue to watch the rows go from pending to ok, and to press Retry on anything stuck.
THE ONE RULE

A queued sale is a real sale. Never ring it again. Every queued sale carries its own identifier, and if it is sent twice the server recognises it and returns the invoice it already made — so a customer can never be charged twice by a replay. But ringing the same basket a second time by hand creates a genuine second sale, and that has to be voided by a manager.

Head-office numbers, point balances and stock levels do not move until the queue has drained. If the dashboard looks light and a store's bar says QUEUED, that is why.

4.11  Clock in and clock out

  1. On the unlock screen choose Clock in, tap your name and key your PIN. Your shift starts.
  2. Tap Shift during the day to start or end a break; the break minutes are tracked separately from worked minutes.
  3. At the end, choose Clock out on the unlock screen and key your PIN. Clocking out never opens the till.

Clocking needs a connection — it writes to the server immediately. If the till is offline, clock in as soon as it is back and tell your manager the real time you started.

4.12  End of day — the X and Z report

Both live on the Shift screen. An X report is a look at the day so far and changes nothing. A Z report is the close: you count the drawer and record the variance.

  1. Open Shift. The day summary shows Gross, Net sales, Tax, Invoices, Avg ticket, the Tenders broken down by cash and card, and Device sales.
  2. Read it during the day as often as you like — that is the X report.
  3. At close, count the drawer and key the total into Counted cash under Cash drawer close.
  4. The screen shows the Variance against what the till expected — cash taken minus change given. Investigate anything that is not small.
  5. Tap Print for the paper copy.

Returns and voids are already netted off; you do not subtract them yourself. Before you close, check the Queue is empty — an unsent sale is not in the numbers yet.

SECTION 5

Store manager — how to

Everything in Section 4, plus the six things only you can do. You are scoped to your own store: you will not see another store's anything.

5.1  Approve a price change

Prices are not edited on the till. A price change is a request that head office or your regional approves, and once approved it becomes a pricing rule that applies to your store only.

The Admin desk card is not on your launcher, but the link at the foot of /start opens it — and the desk shows you only your own store's records.

  1. Open the admin desk at /app and create an AWANZ Price Change Request.
  2. Fill in the item, your store, the proposed rate and the dates it should run between. The current rate is filled in for you.
  3. Submit it. The request moves to Pending Approval and head office is notified.
  4. Head Office or Regional approves or rejects it. On approval the platform creates the pricing rule automatically, scoped to your store's warehouse — no other store is affected.
  5. The new price reaches the tills at the next catalogue refresh. Ask your team to pull down to refresh, or reopen the till.

A request whose proposed rate equals the current rate is refused, and so is a zero or negative rate. Cancelling an approved request disables the pricing rule it created.

A DISCOUNT ON ONE SALE IS NOT A PRICE CHANGE

To take money off a single basket, use the line discount on the basket or a coupon code — that needs no approval and is recorded against the sale. A price change request is for a price that should hold for days or weeks.

5.2  Request stock from the warehouse

  1. On the till open Receive. The screen has three parts: From the warehouse (what is on its way), Vendor deliveries (POs), and My requests.
  2. Tap Request from warehouse.
  3. Add item for each line and set the quantity, or search the catalogue.
  4. Add a Reason if it helps Houston prioritise, then Send request.
  5. The request appears in Houston's approval queue immediately. Watch its status under My requests.

The one-tap route: on the Shift screen, anything below its reorder level appears under Low stock with a Request from warehouse button beside it. One tap raises the request at the suggested quantity. Use this — it is faster and it flags the request as “low stock” priority on the warehouse wall.

Houston may cut the quantity or reject the request with a reason, and you will be told either way. A rejected request is not a lost request — read the reason and raise a new one.

5.3  Receive a delivery by scanning

  1. Open Receive on the till. The shipment appears under From the warehouse with what Houston says is in the box.
  2. Tap it to open the count sheet.
  3. Scan each item as you take it out of the box — one scan per unit — or tap the line and key the quantity. The counted number climbs as you go.
  4. Anything that does not match what was sent is highlighted. Do not correct it to make it match: count what is actually there.
  5. If the box is only half unpacked, save a partial count and come back to it. The shipment stays open until you confirm the final count.
  6. Confirm the receipt. The stock moves into your store and is sellable immediately.

Any short, over or damaged line raises a receiving discrepancy that goes back to Houston for them to resolve. That is the audit trail — resolve it honestly rather than adjusting the count.

Vendor purchase orders addressed to your store are received on the same screen, under Vendor deliveries (POs).

5.4  Run a cycle count

  1. Open Cycle count from the till (also reachable from the Shift screen).
  2. Tap New count and choose what you are counting.
  3. For quantity items, key the counted number per line. For serialised items, scan each serial label; the screen tracks Serials scanned against Remaining.
  4. Anything you scan that was not expected shows as Unexpected; anything expected that you did not find shows as Unaccounted.
  5. Tap Review. The screen tells you either “Counted quantities match” and “Every expected serial was scanned”, or it lists the Quantity differences and the Unaccounted serials.
  6. Submit. The count is recorded and, where there are differences, a draft stock reconciliation is raised for head office to check and post.

5.5  Approve a return over the threshold

The till asks for you in exactly two situations: the credit is over $2,500 including tax, or the sale is outside its window — 30 days for a refund, 60 days for an exchange.

  1. The associate reaches the Manager PIN sheet and calls you over.
  2. Check the sale yourself: the original invoice, the reason and the condition of the goods.
  3. Pick your name from the manager list on the sheet and key your own PIN. Never give your PIN to an associate to type.
  4. The return completes and the credit note records that you approved it. That is on the printed return receipt.

If you are the one signed in to the till, the approval is implicit and no sheet opens. Voids also need a manager — and note that a void has no amount limit today, so treat it as a policy matter, not a technical one.

5.6  Manage your associates and reset a PIN

You run your own shop floor: you may create, edit and disable associates in your own store, at associate level, and reset their PINs.

Reset a PIN

  1. The associate has locked themselves out after five wrong attempts, or has forgotten their PIN. Nobody can look the old one up — it does not exist in readable form.
  2. Open the associate's record and use reset PIN. Choose a new 4-digit PIN and tell them privately.
  3. The lockout clears with the reset. They can unlock straight away.

Add or disable an associate

  1. The person needs a site login first — head office creates the user account.
  2. Attach them to your store at Associate level and set their opening PIN.
  3. When someone leaves, set their record to disabled rather than deleting it, so their past sales, commission and shifts stay intact.
WHAT YOU CANNOT DO — BY DESIGN

You cannot appoint another manager, move somebody between stores, reset another manager's PIN, or give anyone a role above your own. Those are head-office actions. The platform enforces this at four separate levels, so there is no way around it and no point trying — an attempt returns a permission error and is written to the security log.

SECTION 6

Warehouse admin — how to

Houston, HOU-WH. You are the only role that can approve a store's request, pick it, buy a label and ship it. You cannot sell.

6.1  Approve, edit the quantity, or reject

  1. Open /warehouse. The Replenishment requests tab lists everything waiting, with the store, the lines, the age of the request and its priority.
  2. Open a request. You see what the store asked for and what is on hand at HOU-WH beside it.
  3. Approve it as asked, or edit the quantity per line first if you cannot fill it in full and then approve.
  4. Or tap Reject… and give a rejection reason. The store manager is notified with your reason — write something they can act on.
  5. On approval the platform raises an AWANZ Shipment in Pending, and the wall immediately prints the packing list.

6.2  Pick, pack, buy the cheapest label, ship

Pick

  1. Open the shipment. The Pick list names the bin and the on-hand quantity for every line.
  2. Tick each line as you pull it. When they are all ticked the sheet says All picked.

Pack

  1. Build the parcels. Add parcel for each box and confirm its weight and dimensions.
  2. Weights come from the item records plus the box, and anything missing falls back to a conservative default so a rate can always be quoted — check anything unusual by hand. A four-foot bong is not a default box.
  3. Save & mark Packed.

Buy the label

  1. Tap for rates. Carrier services come back priced by weight and destination — on this site, eight of them.
  2. The cheapest is already selected. Toggle Fastest, or pick any row, if the store needs it sooner.
  3. Buy it. The tracking number is stored on the shipment and the wall prints the label.
A LABEL IS MONEY — YOU ONLY GET ONE

The platform refuses to buy a second label for a shipment that already has one. If you genuinely need to replace it, use the explicit replace option: the voided label's carrier, service and tracking are written to the shipment's notes so you can find them — and you still have to refund the unused label in the carrier's own dashboard. The platform cannot do that for you.

Ship

  1. Tap Ship when the parcel leaves the building.
  2. The stock moves out of HOU-WH into that store's In Transit location — so it is never “nowhere”. It only lands in the store when someone there counts it in.
  3. Tracking refreshes on its own; the shipment shows its status on the desk and the wall.

6.3  The 55-inch wall and its auto-printing

/warehouse-wall is the board for the packing floor. Five columns, left to right:

ColumnWhat is in it

  • Cards are ordered by priority first — Urgent, then Low stock — and then by how long they have been waiting, so the oldest is on top.
  • Every card carries an age timer. It turns amber at 4 hours and red at 24. A red card means a store has been waiting a day.
  • A newly approved shipment flashes and sounds so the floor notices without watching.
  • Tap a card to Pick, Pack, Buy a label, Print or Ship without leaving the wall.

Auto-printing

The wall prints two documents with nobody touching a dialog: the packing list when a shipment is approved, and the carrier label when a label is bought. For that to work the machine driving the wall must run Chrome in kiosk-printing mode and have a default printer set.

THE TWO-PRINTER LIMITATION — KNOW THIS BEFORE BUYING HARDWARE

Kiosk printing always goes to the machine's default printer, and Chrome offers no way to pick a different printer per document. So one machine cannot send packing lists to the laser and labels to the 4×6 thermal printer.

The recommended answer is two machines: the wall PC's default printer is the laser (packing lists), and a second small PC at the pack bench runs its own kiosk browser with the thermal printer as its default. The alternative is to leave label auto-printing off and press Print label by hand, choosing the thermal printer in the normal dialog.

6.4  Discrepancies, cancellations and stuck shipments

  • A store reports a short. Their count raises a Receiving discrepancy on your desk. Open it, work out what happened and Resolve it with a reason. That reason is the audit trail — do not delete the row.
  • A shipment is stuck in Pending. Nobody has picked it. The wall's timer is telling you: amber at 4 hours, red at 24.
  • Rates come back empty. Check the store has a complete address including ZIP, and that the carrier account is configured. If switching to the simulated carrier makes rates appear, the problem is the carrier account, not the platform.
  • A shipment has to be cancelled. Cancelling puts the store's request back to Pending Approval, cancels the stock document behind it and notifies the store. Approve it again to raise a fresh shipment, or reject it with a reason.
  • Nothing prints. Confirm the browser is running with kiosk printing — not merely kiosk mode — that a default printer is set for that user, and that auto-printing is switched on in settings.

7.7  Promotions and coupons

  • Coupons are AWANZ Coupon records: single-use or multi-use, optionally bound to one customer and optionally restricted to an item group. The associate types or scans the code at the till, and the till checks it against the basket before it is accepted.
  • The promotion calendar holds one row per month with that month's rules and featured items; the platform sends it as a campaign on the 1st.
  • Measure both with the Promotion Performance report.
TELL THE STORES BEFORE YOU CHANGE ANYTHING

Tills read promotions, coupons and reward tiers from the platform, so a change made here is live at the next catalogue refresh — which may be mid-shift. Associates will be asked about it at the counter before you have finished writing the e-mail.

7.8  Campaigns

  1. Create an AWANZ Campaign in the admin desk: a title, a channel (Email, SMS, Event or Private viewing), a send date, an optional coupon and a cost.
  2. Build the segment from tier, store, customer signal, item or item group — the conditions are combined, and leaving them blank means everyone. Preview the count before you send.
  3. Add featured products if the campaign is about specific items; that turns on item-level attribution.
  4. Export the segment as CSV for your e-mail tool, or push it to a mailing group.
  5. Touches come back automatically from a connected provider through a signed webhook, or you can record them by hand for an event guest list.
  6. Attribution runs nightly: the most recent touch within 14 days of a sale is credited as direct, anything else within 30 days as an assist, and a campaign that featured an item in the basket is credited for those lines specifically.
  7. Read the result in Campaign Performance.
SECTION 8

CloudChaserz Rewards

What to say at the counter: the programme in the customer's words, and the five things staff get asked about.

THE PROGRAMME, IN ONE BREATH

“Earn 1 point for every $1 you spend. Redeem your points for money off at the counter, get a birthday gift, hear about every monthly promotion and new arrival first, enter product giveaways and receive exclusive event invites.”

QuestionWhat to say
“How do I earn?” $1 = 1 point, on what you spend on goods. Points are worked out before sales tax, so the rate is the same in every store.
“What are they worth?” $5 off at 100 points. $10 off at 200. $15 off at 300. One reward per transaction.
“Do they expire?” Not while the account is active.
“Do I need a card?” No. Your points are tied to you — your phone number or your e-mail — so there is nothing to lose. Just give us your number.
“What else do I get?” A birthday discount — 15% off, sent to you a week before your birthday and good for 30 days. Every monthly promotion first. New arrivals the week they land. Giveaway entries, one for every $25 you spend. And invitations to in-store events.

8.1  Sign someone up at the till

  1. Open Client on the till and tap New client.
  2. Take their name, mobile number and e-mail. Ask for their birthday — say why: “so we can send you your birthday discount.”
  3. Ask, out loud, whether they want to hear from you by e-mail and by text, and set the two consent switches to whatever they actually said. Do not tick them by default.
  4. Save, then Attach to sale. They start earning on the basket in front of you.
  5. Their client number is on the receipt. Tell them they can quote that number, or just their phone number, next time.

8.2  Sign up on /rewards

Customers can join themselves at https://cloudchaserz.frappe.cloud/rewards — the join form takes name, mobile, e-mail, birthday, a home store and the two consent boxes. The page also lists the giveaways and events running right now, which is the reason to point people at it. They can also join on the client display at the counter while you are ringing the sale.

If someone signs up online with a phone number or e-mail we already have, the page gives a friendly acknowledgement and changes nothing on the existing record. That is deliberate — it stops anyone rewriting a stranger's account. If a customer says “I signed up but nothing happened,” look them up at the till: they were probably already a member.

8.3  Redeem at the counter

  1. Attach the customer before going to Pay.
  2. The Redeem sheet shows only the rewards they can afford right now, and how many points away the next one is. If nothing is listed, they are under 100 points — tell them how close they are.
  3. Tap the tier. The basket total drops by $5, $10 or $15.
  4. Take the remaining balance as normal. One reward per transaction, and a reward can never be bigger than the bill.

8.4  When something comes back

Returning a sale takes the points back with it. Say it plainly: “the points you earned on that item come off again.” Two things to reassure them about:

  • The balance never goes below zero.
  • A reward they already used comes back when the sale it was used on is returned.

Every receipt prints the numbers, so you can always show the customer rather than argue:

ON EVERY MEMBER'S RECEIPT

POINTS EARNED             173
POINTS BALANCE            423
NEXT REWARD     $10 AT 200 PTS
GIVEAWAY ENTRIES              6

8.5  What not to promise

  • Do not quote a reward that is not on the screen. The till reads the tiers from the platform, and head office can change them.
  • Do not stack rewards. One tier per transaction.
  • Do not promise a reward larger than the bill — it will be refused.
  • Do not promise a giveaway result. Winners are drawn from a recorded seed so the draw can be audited and reproduced; nobody at a store picks the winner.
SECTION 9

Troubleshooting

The nine things that actually happen, what each one means, and what to do about it.

What you seeWhat it meansWhat to do
The PIN is refused Almost always the wrong name is selected on the unlock screen. The two associate PINs are the same in every store, so the PIN only works against the right person. Go back, tap your own name, key the PIN again. Read the message: “incorrect PIN” means try again; “PIN locked after too many failed attempts” means five wrong tries have locked it and a manager must reset it (5.6). Guessing is also rate limited, so slow down rather than hammering it.
The catalogue has not loaded The device has never had the catalogue, or its cache was cleared. Tiles are blank or the search finds nothing. Check the top bar. If it says ONLINE, pull down to refresh, or go to Settings → Catalog → Refresh cache and wait. If it says OFFLINE, the device has nothing to load from — get a connection, then load the catalogue before opening the store. Load the catalogue on a fresh device while you still have signal.
No network The top bar reads OFFLINE. The till is still fully usable for cash sales. Keep selling and take cash. You cannot charge a card, clock in or out, look up a sale for a return, or work web orders. See 4.10 for the whole list. Tell your manager; check the store's router and the tablet's Wi-Fi.
“OFFLINE · 3 QUEUED” Three completed sales are waiting on the device to be sent. They are real sales — they are just not on the server yet. Do not ring them again. When the connection returns the queue sends itself; open Queue to watch. Anything stuck has a Retry button. If a row still fails, note the time and total and call your manager — do not discard it. Head-office numbers and point balances will not move until the queue is empty, so clear it before you close the day.
The printer is not responding The receipt route failed. The till tries the reader's printer, then the counter printer over the network, then the browser. Check the obvious first: paper, lid closed, power. Then Settings → Printer — the printer's IP address must be right, and the receipt route should be Automatic. Use Test reader print to see which route is failing. As a stop-gap, print from the browser dialog or e-mail the receipt to the customer (4.7). The sale is complete either way — a printing problem never costs you the sale.
The card reader is not found The device is not paired to a reader, the reader is off the Wi-Fi, or someone picked a reader that is not on this counter. Check the reader is powered and on the store Wi-Fi. Then Settings → Card reader and pick the right one — each store has more than one registered, e.g. Counter 1 · V660p and Counter 2 · S710. The choice is remembered per device. Take cash while you sort it out.
An age-restricted item is blocked The check returned Underage, Expired, Unreadable or Declined. The item never entered the basket. Underage or expired: the sale does not happen. Remove the item and say so politely; the minimum age is 21 everywhere and there is no override. Unreadable is different — the barcode did not scan. Use Try another ID, or key the date of birth by hand from the front of the licence. Never wave an item through because the scanner is being difficult.
“You don't have access to that store” You are signed in as someone attached to a different store, or the device is still set to a store you no longer work in. Every screen is scoped to one store on purpose. Check the name in the corner is yours. On the till, Settings → Change boutique to your own store; if your store is not in the list, your account is attached elsewhere and head office has to move it — a store manager cannot. If you have genuinely transferred, ask head office to re-point your record.
The dashboard looks empty Either nothing has sold yet today, or a store's sales are sitting in an offline queue. Look at the store cards: a store showing NO SALE YET · OFFLINE has not checked in. Ring one sale anywhere and the board moves within a second. If a card shows queued sales, the till has them and has not sent them.
WHEN TO ESCALATE

Anything that stops you taking money, anything where a customer may have been charged twice, and anything that looks like data from another store, goes to your manager immediately and then to head office. Note the time, the till, the invoice number if there is one, and what the screen said — those four things make the difference between a fix and a guess.

The web shop does not sell age-restricted items and that default must not be changed without counsel and a compliant carrier programme. None of this is legal advice — it is a starting point for a conversation with the company's attorney.
